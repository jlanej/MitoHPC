Deletions tested: del4977 = the MITOMAP common deletion (m.8470–13447, mediated by a 13 bp direct repeat); NONREP = a non-repeat ~5 kb deletion (m.6000–10998, no flanking repeat), included to show recovery is not specific to the repeat-mediated common deletion; HP_ARTIFACT = a deletion placed in the control-region poly-C homopolymer tract (m.305–965) — the artifact class that dominates real cohorts, used here as a hard negative; ORIGIN = an origin-crossing deletion that must be suppressed.

Overview

Mitochondrial deletions are a clinically important but technically awkward variant class: the genome is small, circular, and present at extreme, uneven copy number, and its most artifact-prone region (the control region) generates the dominant false positives. We therefore quantified, rather than merely demonstrated, how well the MitoHPC deletion caller performs, using a two-arm in-silico benchmark over a heteroplasmy × depth grid. The simulated arm builds wild-type + deletion mixtures at a known mutant fraction (clean ground truth, the full grid, replicate seeding); the real-spiked arm injects the same deletions into wild-type mitochondrial backgrounds from three 1000 Genomes individuals, so calls are tested against real error, coverage and NUMT structure. Both arms run through the production circular-alignment path. We report detection sensitivity and a PASS limit of detection (following CLSI EP17-A2 in treating the limit as a dose-response rather than a single number), breakpoint and heteroplasmy accuracy, specificity on blanks, and the discrimination and calibration of the per-call confidence score (SVCONF), which exists to separate true deletions from the recurrent control-region artifact.

Principal findings. The caller recovers the common deletion (del4977) and an unrelated non-repeat deletion with a PASS threshold near 8% heteroplasmy at production depth, improving modestly with coverage; the simulated and real-spiked arms agree, indicating the limit reflects coverage and biology rather than an artifact of the simulator. Junction-level detection extends below the PASS threshold by design. The coverage-dosage heteroplasmy estimate is essentially unbiased in clean data down to ~3%, while in real backgrounds it loses sensitivity at the lowest fractions as coverage noise masks a small dosage drop. Specificity is complete on this grid: no PASS call on any of 62 wild-type / origin-suppression negative runs. The confidence score rises monotonically with heteroplasmy, is stable across depth, and cleanly separates true deletions (median SVCONF 42) from the control-region homopolymer artifact (median 9) it is designed to demote; as a ranking score it achieves AUPRC 0.97 against a 0.86 prevalence baseline, and although the raw 0–100 value is not itself a probability, a simple isotonic recalibration maps it to one (calibration error 0.43 → 0.05).

2. Limit-of-detection surface — detection & PASS rate (CLSI EP17-A2)

Detection rate = the fraction of replicates in which the deletion's junction was found at the right place; PASS rate = the fraction the pipeline reports as a confident call. We give a surface for both (Figure 1) because they answer different questions — what the caller can SEE vs what it will confidently REPORT — and detection reaches lower heteroplasmy than PASS by design. The empirical per-cell rates are the primary read-out and localize the PASS limit to ~8% heteroplasmy on this reduced grid; Figure 1 shows the point estimates, and their Wilson 95% confidence intervals are drawn as the error bars in Figure 2 and tabulated per cell in lod_cells.tsv. The probit/logistic dose-response fits (Figure 2; numbers in lod_fits.tsv) are shown for completeness but are unstable here because the response is near-separable, so we treat them as supporting only (see §8).

8. Limitations and next steps

These results come from the tractable --quick grid and should be read with its limits in mind, each of which has a clear remedy:

  • The PASS limit of detection is localized, not tightly bounded. With 8 replicates per level the dose-response is near-separable (PASS jumps from ~0 below 5% to ~100% by 10%), which both widens the binomial confidence intervals and destabilizes the parametric (probit/logistic) LoD — its point estimate is reported only as support for the ~8% empirical transition, not as a precise value. Next: the --full grid (heteroplasmy sampled densely at 5–10%, ≥30 replicates per level, all five depths) bounds LoD50/LoD95 with bootstrap intervals, and a separation-robust (Firth-penalized) fit replaces the unstable GLM.
  • The real-background arm is narrow. Concordance with simulation is shown for del4977 spiked into a single background; the non-repeat and origin-distal deletions are simulation-only. Next: spike the non-repeat (and an origin-distal) deletion into all three backgrounds so the repeat-independence and breakpoint-class claims carry a real-data anchor.
  • The confidence score is calibrated to this evaluation's class mix. SVCONF weights and bands are expert-set, and the isotonic map that turns the rank into a probability is specific to the present true-deletion / artifact census. Next: re-tune the weights on the full grid and fit the recalibration map on a held-out split, then publish it as the operating confidence→probability mapping.
  • Scope is deletions. Duplications, inversions, insertions, and true origin-spanning deletions are not evaluated; the last are conservatively suppressed (counted here only as a no-false-call control). Next: add a duplication / origin-spanning truth panel as the caller's structural scope expands.
  • The high-depth artifact regime is under-probed. The dominant real-cohort false positive emerged mainly at full (8–22k×) depth; this grid caps at 4000×. Next: add a high-depth negative panel to confirm the fragile penalty holds where the artifact is strongest.
